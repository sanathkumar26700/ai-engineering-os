# LinkedIn Profile Optimizer

## Philosophy First

Before touching any section, internalize this:

**A LinkedIn profile is presuasion material.** Before you ever send a DM, before a recruiter reads your message, they've already made a judgment. Your profile either removes their risk of engaging with you — or it adds to it.

Four things combine to decide if someone replies to you:
1. Your LinkedIn profile
2. The content you post
3. The DM message you sent
4. Who you sent it to

This skill optimizes #1. Every word, every title, every line of the about section has one job: make them feel stupid for NOT reaching out.

**The Premium Test:** When someone opens your profile, does it *scream* premiumness in the first glance? If you have to think about it — the answer is no.

---

## Step 1: Gather Profile Information

Before writing anything, ask the student to share:

1. **Current headline** (the line under their name)
2. **Current about/summary section**
3. **Banner text** (if any — or describe what's currently there)
4. **Top 2-3 experience titles** (just the job titles and companies, not full descriptions)
5. **Years of experience** in any relevant field (AI, ML, SWE, data, research, etc.)
6. **What they want to be known for** — what's the one thing? (e.g., agentic AI, core ML, AI + marketing, MLOps)
7. **Who they want to attract** — recruiters? founders? collaborators? (US/EU market assumed by default)
8. **Any notable associations** — universities, research labs, companies, funded startups, cohorts, open source contributions

If they paste their full profile text, extract these yourself and confirm before proceeding.

---

## Step 2: Diagnose Before You Rewrite

Read `references/diagnosis-rubric.md` to score their profile before making changes.

Call out specific problems directly — no sugarcoating. Common issues:

- Headline uses "student" or "intern" → kills authority instantly
- About section reads like a resume summary, not a transformation promise
- Banner is blank, generic, or inconsistent with the rest of the profile
- Open to work badge is on → signals desperation
- Experience titles are too literal ("Data Science Intern") vs. authoritative ("AI Research @ [Lab Name]")
- Profile photo is casual, dark, or low-quality
- Posts in the feed look unprofessional, meme-y, or inconsistent
- Design inconsistency — blue banner, white background, random colors everywhere
- No featured section or featured section is empty

---

## Step 3: Rewrite Each Section

### Headline

**Goal:** One line that shows who you are + what transformation you bring + a taste of authority.

**Formula options:**
- `[Role] | [Differentiator] | [Social proof or bold claim]`
- `[What you do] for [who] | [years of experience or credibility signal]`
- Contrarian opener: "Not your usual [role]." then follow up with what makes you different

**Rules:**
- Never use "student", "intern", "fresher", "looking for opportunities"
- Replace "intern at X" with "Research at X" or "AI at X" — you're not lying, you're repositioning
- If they have 4+ years experience → "4+ years in [field]" or "Half a decade in [field]"
- Combine old experience with new direction: "Core ML + Agentic AI" if that's where they're headed
- Add a contrarian signal if possible: "building AI before ChatGPT era", "not just an API caller"

**Output:** Give 3 headline variants. Label them by tone (Direct Authority / Contrarian Hook / Transformation-Led).

---

### Banner

**Goal:** One visual statement that communicates your moat — instantly, abstractly, memorably.

**Rules:**
- Should communicate ONE thing: your differentiator
- Use social proof language, even if it's borrowed/synthetic: "Combined valuation of clients: $X" or "Worked with teams building for $Y ARR companies"
- Reference: SBL's combined client valuation = ~$450M → always frame as "half a billion dollars" not "$450 million"
- Consistency: banner colors should match profile photo background and overall visual tone
- Minimalist > complex. If it needs a design degree to make, simplify.
- Tools to suggest: Canva, GPT image gen, Figma for banner creation

**Output:** Write the text/copy for the banner. Flag any design direction (colors, layout style).

---

### About Section

**Goal:** Promise a transformation. Make them feel stupid for not reaching out.

**Rules:**
- Write like you're explaining to a 5th grader — simple words, deep meaning
- Answer the real question every reader has: "Will this person make me money, save me money, or save me time?"
- Offer a transformation, not a description. Not "I know Python and TensorFlow." But "I help teams ship ML systems that cut [X] cost by [Y] — without rewriting their stack."
- The transformation should scare you a little. If it doesn't, it's not bold enough.
- Use your own voice — pull from their writing sample / master doc if they have one
- Avoid: corporate buzzwords, "cross-functional collaboration", "scalable solutions", "passionate about"
- End with a soft call to action or signal of what you're building toward

**Structure (loosely):**
1. Opening hook — who you are in one punchy sentence (your current state or bold claim)
2. What you actually do / have done — specific, not abstract
3. The transformation you bring — framed around making/saving money or time
4. What you're building toward / what kind of work excites you
5. Soft CTA — "DM me if you're building in [X]" or "let's connect if you're working on [Y]"

**Output:** Full rewritten about section (200–300 words max).

---

### Experience Titles

**Goal:** Make every title sound like authority, not obligation.

**Rules:**
- Remove "intern" where possible — replace with the function: "AI Research at [Lab]", "ML Engineer at [Company]"
- Add current tech context to old roles where honest: if they did backend work and now know agents, they can note "...including early AI integration work"
- First two bullet points under each role matter most — most people don't read further
- Titles should trend toward: "AI-native [role]", "ML-focused [function]", "[Field] + AI Engineer"

**Output:** Rewritten titles for their top 2–3 roles.

---

### Featured Section

**Rules:**
- This is prime real estate — treat it like a billboard
- Should contain: best post, best project, Dev.to article, portfolio link, or a result/case study
- Design consistency: thumbnails should be same color palette
- Don't leave it empty — ever

**Output:** Suggest 3 things they should put in featured, based on what they shared.

---

### Profile Photo

**Not rewritable, but flag if:**
- Background is dark/cluttered
- Photo is casual (gym selfie, group photo, blurry)
- Face isn't lit well

**Suggestion:** Use AI headshot tools (Try It On AI, Aragon, Remini) for professional headshots without a photoshoot. Ring light + neutral background + good framing is enough for a DIY version.

---

## Step 4: Trim and Stack

After rewriting, apply the final filter:

> "Trim everything that reduces premiumness. Stack everything that adds it."

Go through their existing posts (if visible) and flag anything that looks unprofessional, off-brand, or inconsistent. Advise them to remove or archive those posts.

Apply the "Open to Work" rule: if it's on — tell them to turn it off. Silent applications are fine. Public desperation signals hurt more than they help.

---

## Step 5: Output Format

Deliver in this order:
1. **Diagnosis** — 3-5 specific things that are hurting the profile right now
2. **Headline** — 3 variants (labeled)
3. **Banner copy** — text + brief design direction
4. **About section** — full rewrite
5. **Experience titles** — rewritten top 2–3
6. **Featured section** — 3 suggestions
7. **Quick wins** — 3 things they can fix in the next 30 minutes (photo, open to work, removing bad posts)
