# Authority Language Reference

## The Synthetic Authority Principle

You don't need to lie. You need to *frame* what's true in the most powerful way.

**Example:**
- Weak: "The combined valuation of SBL clients is around $450 million"
- Strong: "The combined valuation of SBL clients is half a billion dollars"

Same fact. One frames it like a spreadsheet cell. The other frames it like a headline.

Always ask: what's the most powerful *true* framing of this fact?

---

## Social Proof Borrowing

When you don't have direct social proof, you borrow it from proximity:

| Situation | How to Frame It |
|---|---|
| You're in a cohort with top practitioners | "Learning under people who've built for $X ARR companies" |
| You worked at a lab affiliated with a university | "Research at [Lab Name], [University]" — not "intern" |
| Your company's clients are well-funded | "Building AI for companies backed by [VC names]" |
| You contributed to an open source project | "Contributor to [Project Name] — used by X engineers" |
| You attended a competitive program | "Selected for [Program] — [acceptance rate or cohort size]" |

---

## Power Words vs. Weak Words

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Intern | Research at / Engineer at / Built at |
| Student | Early-stage researcher / Learning practitioner |
| Exploring | Building |
| Interested in | Focused on |
| I know Python | I ship production Python |
| Looking for opportunities | Building in [X] — open to the right collaboration |
| Passionate about AI | 3 years deep in ML systems before ChatGPT existed |
| I have experience with | I've shipped / I've built / I've scaled |
| Worked on | Led / Designed / Shipped / Rebuilt |

## The Half-Decade Trick

Never say "4 years." Say "4+ years" or "half a decade."
Never say "$450 million." Say "close to half a billion" or "~$500M."
Never say "a few clients." Say "a portfolio of clients" or "a growing roster."

## Title Formulas That Work

For AI/ML roles:
- `[X]+ years in ML | Now building [Y]`
- `Core ML → Agentic AI | [Company or Lab]`
- `AI Engineer | [Differentiator] | Before the ChatGPT wave`
- `Not just an API caller. [X] years in [field].`
- `AI-Native [Role] | [Social proof hook]`

For SWE with AI pivot:
- `SWE → AI Systems | [Years] experience, now shipping agents`
- `Backend engineer building AI-native systems`
- `Full-stack → AI-first | [Company] | [Years]`

For data/ML engineers:
- `AI-Native Data Engineer | [Years] in [stack]`
- `MLOps + Agentic AI | [Lab or Company]`
- `Data → Intelligence | [X] years in production ML`

## The Scary Transformation Test

A good transformation promise in your About section should slightly scare you.

If you write "I help teams ship better ML models" — that doesn't scare you. It's safe. It's also forgettable.

If you write "I help ML teams cut their inference costs by 40% without touching their model architecture" — that's specific, bold, and slightly scary to promise. That's the sweet spot.

The test: Does writing this commit you to something? Does it feel like a real promise with accountability? If yes — use it.

## The Y Combinator Frame

Study how YC-backed companies introduce themselves:

> "We are backed by Y Combinator, the fund behind Airbnb, Stripe, and Coinbase."

They're not lying. They're selecting the most powerful true facts and sequencing them for maximum credibility transfer.

Apply this to your own profile:
- Don't say "I did some ML work at a research lab."
- Say "AI Research at [Lab Name] — the same team that [published X / built Y / was cited by Z]."

You didn't do anything wrong. You chose better words.
