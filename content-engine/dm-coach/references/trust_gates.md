# Trust Gates Reference — G1 through G6

The Trust Gate system maps the buyer's psychological journey. Each gate is a question the
buyer is asking — consciously or not — before they're willing to move forward.

**Core Rule: You cannot unlock G3 before G2. You cannot unlock G5 before G4. Gates are sequential.**

**Diagnostic Rule: Always assume the lowest plausible gate. If you're not sure between G2 and G3, call it G2.**

---

## G1 — "Are you real?"
**The question:** Is this person actually a human with real intent, or a bot / template blaster?

**What triggers this gate:**
- Cold outreach (no prior relationship)
- Generic opening messages
- Profile that looks like a salesperson
- Message feels copy-pasted

**What it looks like in the DM thread:**
- No reply at all
- One-word reply ("sure", "ok", "thanks")
- Polite brush-off ("I'll keep it in mind")
- Asking a basic clarifying question that tests if you're paying attention

**What keeps them at G1:**
- Following up too fast
- Sending a second pitch before earning a reply to the first
- Template language ("I noticed you're in [industry]...")
- Over-professionalism that feels automated

**What moves them to G2:**
- A message that proves you actually looked at them
- Something specific to their profile, recent post, or company situation
- A genuine observation, not a compliment
- Brevity — short messages feel more human than long ones

**Allowed moves at G1:** Observation message, Validation drop, Profile-specific hook

---

## G2 — "Are you different?"
**The question:** You seem real. But are you just another person selling the same thing as everyone else?

**What triggers this gate:**
- They've heard similar pitches before
- Their industry gets a lot of outreach
- They've been burned by a service like yours before
- They ask: "How is this different from X?" or "What makes you different?"

**What it looks like in the DM thread:**
- Mild engagement but low energy
- "I've seen things like this before"
- Asking about competitors or comparison
- Replies are short — they're testing if you'll say something interesting
- They've replied but they're not leaning in

**What keeps them at G2:**
- Listing features/benefits (everyone does this)
- Social proof that looks like everyone else's (revenue claims, client counts)
- Over-explaining your offer
- Enthusiasm without substance

**What moves them to G3:**
- A specific POV that most people in your space don't hold
- Naming a problem in their industry that others dance around
- A screenshot or result that's genuinely unusual
- Saying something true that slightly surprises them

**Allowed moves at G2:** Screenshot (unusual result), Specific POV message, Contrarian observation about their industry

---

## G3 — "Do you get me?"
**The question:** OK, you're different. But do you actually understand MY situation specifically?

**What triggers this gate:**
- They've engaged enough to be curious
- But they haven't felt personally understood yet
- They're wondering if your solution actually fits their context

**What it looks like in the DM thread:**
- They share a specific detail about their situation
- They ask "does this work for [their specific context]?"
- They mention a constraint, challenge, or nuance
- They're engaging but still holding back — one foot in, one foot out

**What keeps them at G3:**
- Giving generic answers to specific questions
- Moving to pitch mode as soon as they show curiosity
- Answering their question without reflecting their situation back
- Case studies from irrelevant industries or company sizes

**What moves them to G4:**
- A case study that mirrors their specific situation (industry, role, company size, problem)
- Demonstrating you understand the nuance they raised
- Asking a question that shows you've processed their context — not a discovery question, a *reflection* question
- NOT pitching. Showing understanding first.

**Allowed moves at G3:** Matched case study, Reflection question, Context-specific observation

---

## G4 — "Is it safe to explore?"
**The question:** I'm interested. But if I engage more deeply, will I get pressured?

**What triggers this gate:**
- They've had bad experiences with sales pressure
- They feel like they're getting close to a pitch
- They're decision-makers who hate feeling sold to
- The conversation has been going well — and now they're bracing

**What it looks like in the DM thread:**
- They were warm, then suddenly slowed down
- They ask vague questions that delay commitment ("what does the process look like?")
- They say "let me think about it"
- They've gone a bit quiet after a promising message
- They're orbiting — interested but not stepping forward

**What keeps them at G4:**
- Suggesting a call before they've asked
- Any form of urgency ("I only have 2 spots left")
- Over-enthusiasm when they show interest
- Treating their curiosity as a buying signal and accelerating

**What moves them to G5:**
- Explicitly reducing pressure ("no rush, just wanted to share this")
- A resource that gives value with no ask attached
- Letting them set the pace
- A message that acknowledges the relationship before the business

**Allowed moves at G4:** Resource drop, Pressure-reducing message, Permission-based next step ("would it be useful if I sent you X?")

---

## G5 — "Is this worth the risk?"
**The question:** I want this. But is the outcome certain enough to justify what I have to give up (money, time, status, switching cost)?

**What triggers this gate:**
- They're seriously considering it
- But the gap between desire and decision is risk
- P(outcome) is the key variable here — do they believe it'll work for them?

**What it looks like in the DM thread:**
- Specific objections about ROI, timeline, or results
- "What happens if it doesn't work?"
- "Can you guarantee X?"
- "We tried something like this before and it didn't work"
- They want to say yes but need a reason to believe

**What keeps them at G5:**
- Feature-heavy responses to outcome-based fears
- Guarantees that feel too good to be true
- Pushing for a decision before the risk feels managed
- Responding to the surface objection instead of the underlying fear

**What moves them to G6:**
- A case study with near-identical context to their situation (same role, same fear, same outcome)
- Specific numbers that make P(outcome) feel real
- Framing risk as asymmetric ("the downside is X, the upside is Y")
- Social proof from someone they trust or identify with

**Allowed moves at G5:** High-specificity case study, Risk-reframe message, Outcome evidence (screenshot with context)

---

## G6 — Long-Term Trust
**The question:** Is this a relationship or a transaction?

**What triggers this gate:**
- They've bought or are very close to buying
- They're evaluating whether this is someone they want a long relationship with
- They're thinking about referrals, renewals, or future work

**What it looks like in the DM thread:**
- Post-purchase or post-call follow-up context
- They're checking in after a result
- They're sharing your work with someone else
- They ask about other things you do

**What moves them deeper into G6:**
- Consistent follow-through with no agenda
- Sharing value after the sale
- Remembering details from earlier conversations
- Being genuinely interested in their progress, not just their account

## Gate Detection Cheat Sheet

| Signal | Likely Gate |
|--------|------------|
| No reply / ghost | G1 |
| One-word reply | G1 |
| "How is this different from X?" | G2 |
| Low energy engagement | G2 |
| "Does this work for [my situation]?" | G3 |
| Sharing a specific detail | G3 |
| Slowed down after warm exchange | G4 |
| "Let me think about it" | G4 |
| Specific ROI / result objection | G5 |
| "We tried this before" | G5 |
| "Can you guarantee X?" | G5 |
