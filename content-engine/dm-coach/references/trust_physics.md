# Trust Physics — Decay Mechanics & System Failures

This file defines the underlying physics of why trust breaks in DM conversations.
Use it to diagnose what went wrong in a thread, not just what gate the buyer is at.

---

## The Core Equation

```
TU = P(outcome) × Value – Risk
```

**TU (Trust Utility):** The buyer's net motivation to move forward. When TU > 0 and rising, they move. When TU is falling or negative, they ghost, stall, or object.

**P(outcome):** Their subjective probability that this will work for them specifically. Not "does it work" — "will it work for me, with my constraints, in my context."

**Value:** What they believe they'll gain. Note: this is their perceived value, not your stated value. The gap between those two is where most deals die.

**Risk:** Everything they're giving up or exposing themselves to: money, time, reputation, decision-making effort, switching cost, being wrong, being pressured.

**Key insight:** Increasing Value without reducing Risk often increases TU briefly then collapses it. The buyer feels excited then scared.

## The 5 Trust Decay Mechanisms

### 1. ☢️ Trust Decay (Baseline)

Trust is not neutral. It is always either building or decaying. There is no plateau.

Every message in a DM thread either:
- **Slows decay** (builds familiarity, coherence, understanding)
- **Accelerates decay** (adds pressure, feels incongruent, jumps gates)

Most sales tactics are decay accelerators dressed as trust builders.

**Common decay accelerators:**
- Follow-up messages sent too quickly
- Asking for commitment before building belief
- Explaining more when they go quiet (they went quiet because of the explanation)
- Sending "just checking in" messages
- Switching to formal language after casual exchange

**Decay is exponential, not linear.** One wrong move at G4 can erase weeks of trust building. This is why gate-skipping is catastrophic.

---

### 2. 📡 Modality Mismatch

Different communication channels carry different trust risk levels.

```
Text (DM)      → Safest. Async. Low exposure.
Voice note     → Medium risk. Tone revealed. Harder to seem natural.
Video message  → High risk. Energy, environment, coherence all visible.
Video call     → Highest risk. Full bandwidth. Every micro-signal exposed.
In-person      → Highest stakes. Everything amplified.
```

**The mismatch failure:** Moving the buyer to a higher-bandwidth channel before their trust is calibrated for it.

If a buyer's trust is at G2 in text DMs, a video call will feel overwhelming. They're not ready to expose themselves to that level of real-time judgment.

**What this means in practice:**
- Don't push for a call at G1–G3
- If they suggest a call, accept — don't push for it before they do
- Voice notes can feel more human or more salesy depending on tone — use with caution
- Video messages should only be used once coherence is established

**Mismatch detection in threads:**
- They agreed to a call then cancelled or rescheduled repeatedly → premature modality push
- They went cold after you suggested a call → G4 not reached yet

---

### 3. 🎁 Value Stacking Paradox

**The intuitive belief:** More value = more trust = more conversions.

**The actual physics:** Past a certain point, more value creates *more resistance*, not less.

Why? Because value creates **trust debt**.

When someone receives a lot of value for free, they feel:
1. Obligated (they owe you something)
2. Pressured (the value feels like a setup for a pitch)
3. Skeptical (if it's this good for free, what's the catch?)

**The debt threshold:** Once a buyer feels like they owe you more than they're comfortable with, they start **avoiding you** to avoid the discomfort of the debt.

**What this looks like in DM threads:**
- You sent a detailed resource, a case study, AND a framework in one message
- They responded warmly to the first thing, then went quiet after the full package
- They said "thanks so much, really helpful" and then ghosted
- You added bonuses to your offer and the conversation slowed down

**The fix:** One value asset per trust gate. Give one thing. Wait. See if they engage. Then give the next thing only if they're still moving.

---

### 4. 🧩 Coherence Collapse

Trust is built on coherence — the sense that who you are matches how you sell matches what you deliver.

When the buyer perceives a gap in coherence, trust collapses faster than it decayed.

**The three coherence gaps:**

1. **Content voice ≠ DM voice**
   - Their content is casual and honest, their DM is formal and pitch-y
   - Buyer thinks: "This isn't who I thought I was talking to"
   
2. **DM voice ≠ Call voice**
   - They seemed laid-back in DMs, high-energy and salesy on the call
   - Buyer thinks: "They put on a different personality when it's time to sell"

3. **Claimed identity ≠ Demonstrated behavior**
   - They say "I never pressure people" then send a third follow-up in 48 hours
   - They say "I only work with a few clients" but the message feels mass-produced

**Coherence collapse signals in threads:**
- They were warm, then suddenly went cold after a call or voice note
- They started questioning things they hadn't questioned before
- They ask questions that suggest they're fact-checking you
- Energy dropped sharply after you "turned on" selling mode

**The fix:** Maintain the same voice, pace, and energy across every touchpoint. The call should sound like the DMs. The offer should feel like the content.

---

### 5. 🌀 Persuasion Death Spiral

The trap most reps fall into:

```
Conversion drops
→ Add more tactics (urgency, social proof, follow-ups)
→ Trust drops faster
→ Conversion drops more
→ Add even more tactics
→ Full decay
```

Each tactic adds a tiny amount of short-term pressure and a large amount of long-term resistance.

**Why it's a spiral:** The tactics that feel like they should work (urgency, scarcity, more follow-ups) are the exact mechanisms that signal to the buyer's nervous system: "This person needs me more than I need them." And once that dynamic is established, trust collapses.

**Spiral detection signals:**
- Multiple follow-ups in the thread without a response
- Escalating language in follow-ups ("I wanted to follow up on my last message, and also share this, and also wanted to mention...")
- Adding new proof, new bonuses, new angles on each follow-up
- The buyer's responses are getting shorter or more dismissive over time
- Rep is working harder while buyer is engaging less

**The only way out of the spiral:** Stop. Go silent for 5–7 days. Then re-engage with a message that has zero sales energy — something that shows you were thinking of them with no agenda. A resource, an observation, a genuine question about something they mentioned.

## Quick Diagnosis Reference

| Signal in Thread | Decay Mechanism |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Warm → cold after call suggestion | Modality mismatch |
| "Thanks so much!" then ghost | Value stacking paradox |
| Energy drop after voice/video | Modality mismatch + coherence |
| 3+ follow-ups, declining replies | Persuasion death spiral |
| Warmer in comments than DMs | Coherence collapse (DM feels off-brand) |
| Sudden skepticism mid-thread | Coherence collapse signal |
| "Let me think about it" + silence | G4 + risk too high |
| Short replies despite clear interest | Trust debt building |

## The Nervous System Rule

Buyers do not consciously analyze trust.

Their nervous system scans every message for:
1. **Safety** — Is this person going to pressure me?
2. **Coherence** — Does this match who I thought they were?
3. **Agenda** — Are they genuinely interested in me or just in my money?

If any of these three sensors fire a warning, the conscious mind finds a logical reason to delay, object, or ghost — but the *real* reason is nervous system protection.

**This is why logic doesn't fix trust problems.** More data, more proof, more value does not override a nervous system signal. Only consistent, coherent, low-pressure behavior over time rebuilds it.
