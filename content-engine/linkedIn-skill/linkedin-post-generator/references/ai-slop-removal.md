# AI Slop Removal — LinkedIn Edition

LinkedIn's algorithm detects AI-generated content and reduces its reach. These patterns are derived from empirical research on AI "slop" (from the paper *Measuring AI "Slop" in Text*) and real-world platform performance.

## Absolute Banned Words (never use, ever)

If any of these appear in your post, rewrite that sentence from scratch:

- **delve** / **delve into** / **let's delve**

- **testament to** ("a testament to your hard work")

- **leverage** (as a verb — "leverage this opportunity")

- **robust** (used to mean "strong" or "comprehensive")
- **seamless** / **seamlessly**
- **groundbreaking**

- **cutting-edge**
- **innovative** / **innovate** (unless quoting someone)
- **transformative**

## Structural AI Patterns (rewrite these)

### The Rule of Three Parallelism
AI loves listing exactly three things in matching syntax.

**AI:** "This approach is efficient, scalable, and reliable."
**Human:** "This works because it's fast. And it doesn't break at scale."

**AI:** "Whether you're a beginner, an intermediate practitioner, or an expert..."
**Human:** "Doesn't matter if you just started — this applies."

### The "Despite X... However Y" Structure
**AI:** "Despite its limitations, AI has shown remarkable progress. However, challenges remain."
**Human:** "AI is genuinely impressive right now. The limitations are real though — and people aren't talking about them."

### Significance Escalation at End of Paragraph
**AI:** "...underscoring the growing importance of agentic systems in modern enterprise contexts."
**Human:** "...which is why every ML team I talk to is asking about agents."

### The Formal Hedge
**AI:** "It's worth considering that this approach may not be suitable for all use cases."
**Human:** "This won't work for everyone — especially if your data pipeline is already a mess."

### Perfect Grammar Everywhere
Real humans make small mistakes. Fragments. Run-ons. Starting sentences with "And" or "But."

**Add one intentional imperfection per post:**
- A sentence fragment: "Which is exactly the problem."
- Starting with a conjunction: "But here's the thing."
- A casual aside: "(honestly still not sure this was the right call)"
- A colloquial shorthand: "ngl", "tbh", "rn" — sparingly, if it fits your voice


## 🔬 Empirical AI "Slop" Dimensions & Fixes

Scientific analysis categorizes AI "slop" into three main themes: **Information Utility**, **Information Quality**, and **Style Quality**. Here is how they manifest on LinkedIn and how to write around them.

### 1. Information Utility (Density & Relevance)

*   **Low Information Density (IU1 - Filler/Fluff):**
    *   *AI Pattern:* Verbose writing that says almost nothing. Broad introductory throat-clearing and placeholder phrasing.
    *   *AI:* "In today's fast-paced modern world of cutting-edge technology and innovation, it has become increasingly important to consider the various factors..."
    *   *Human:* "Most teams build the wrong features because they don't talk to customers."
    *   *Rule:* Cut any sentence that can be removed without changing the core message. Focus on high-substance density.
*   **Irrelevance (IU2 - Tangential Padding):**
    *   *AI Pattern:* Missing the nuance of the prompt or answering a question with broad, generic summaries instead of direct answers.
    *   *Rule:* Start the hook and body directly with the specific lesson, anecdote, or outcome. No generic background context.

### 2. Information Quality (Factuality & Subjectivity)

*   **"Objectivity Slop" / Detached Voice (IQ2 - Bias/Subjectivity):**
    *   *AI Pattern:* AI defaults to a neutral, third-person, balanced perspective (e.g., "universally beneficial" or "it represents a complex phenomenon with arguments on both sides") when the context requires a personal, opinionated, or subjective point of view.
    *   *AI:* "The economic changes of 2023 were beneficial to some but presented challenges to others."
    *   *Human:* "This algorithm update killed our organic reach. Here is how we adapted."
    *   *Rule:* Avoid neutral fence-sitting. Take a clear, first-person stand based on your lived experience. LinkedIn rewards opinionated builder perspectives.
*   **Hallucinated Authority (IQ1 - Factuality):**
    *   *AI Pattern:* Inventing fake statistics, fake quotes, or attributing research to sound authoritative.
    *   *Rule:* Use real data or rely on personal anecdote rather than fake authoritative claims.

### 3. Style Quality (Structure, Coherence, & Tone)

*   **"Unnatural Naturalness" / Over-polished Fluency (SQ4 - Language Naturalness):**
    *   *AI Pattern:* Grammatically perfect, stiff language that reads like a textbook instead of conversational communication.
    *   *AI:* "The earthen area that formerly held the puddle was now dry."
    *   *Human:* "The puddle dried up."
    *   *Rule:* Write like you are texting a smart colleague. Use fragments, contractions, and natural phrasing.
*   **Outside Observer Tone (SQ7 - Tone):**
    *   *AI Pattern:* Writing that reads like a press release or an outside reporter reporting on the work, rather than an active builder/participant.
    *   *AI:* "The team successfully accomplished their goals due to hard work and dedication."
    *   *Human:* "We worked 80-hour weeks. It sucked, but we shipped the feature on time."
    *   *Rule:* Write from the trenches. Share the messiness, the bugs, and the raw details.
*   **Jargon & Word Complexity (SQ6):**
    *   *AI Pattern:* Overusing rare words, complex jargon, and industry buzzwords to sound smart, which obscures the meaning.
    *   *Rule:* Keep readability at a 5th-grade level. Avoid words like "multifaceted," "complexities," and unnecessary jargon unless explaining a technical concept.
*   **The Rule of Three Parallelism (SQ2 - Templatedness):**
    *   *AI Pattern:* Listing exactly three things in matching syntax.
    *   *AI:* "This approach is efficient, scalable, and reliable."
    *   *Human:* "This works because it's fast. And it doesn't break at scale."
*   **The "Despite X... However Y" Structure:**
    *   *AI Pattern:* Predictable contrast hedging.
    *   *AI:* "Despite its limitations, AI has shown remarkable progress. However, challenges remain."
    *   *Human:* "AI is genuinely impressive right now. The limitations are real though — and people aren't talking about them."

## 🧠 The AI Smell Test

Before posting, read your draft aloud and ask:

1. Could this have been written by anyone? → Bad. Make it specific to your experience.
2. Does every sentence follow perfectly from the last? → Too polished. Break the flow somewhere.
3. Is there any word a 5th grader wouldn't use? → Find a simpler word.
4. Does it end with a meaningful reflection on the broader significance of something? → Cut that. End with a human note.
5. Could you have written this in 3 minutes without thinking? → You probably did. Think more.

## Wikipedia AI Writing Signals (Source Reference)

LinkedIn is believed to use similar signals to Wikipedia's AI cleanup project. Common Wikipedia-flagged AI tells that apply to LinkedIn:

- Overuse of passive voice throughout the piece
- Sentences that explain context that doesn't need explaining ("As is well known...")
- Consistent use of em dashes (—) for dramatic pauses
- Closing with a sentence about legacy, impact, or future importance
- Mentioning "complexities" without specifying what they are
- Using "multifaceted" to avoid specifics
- Adjective clusters: "innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking"

## 📋 Quick Fix Checklist

Before finalizing any LinkedIn post, check:

- [ ] **No words from the banned list** (check for "delve," "landscape," "tapestry," etc.).
- [ ] **No three-part parallel structures** (break them into two items or separate sentences).
- [ ] **No "Despite X, however Y" patterns**.
- [ ] **No significance escalation at paragraph end** (avoid concluding with "underscoring the importance of...").
- [ ] **Active Builder Voice** (no detached "Outside Observer" tone).
- [ ] **Subjective Stance** (you have taken a clear stand, not neutral fence-sitting).
- [ ] **High Density** (removed all filler words, preambles, and fluff).
- [ ] **At least one intentional imperfection** (sentence fragment, conversational aside, "tbh", "ngl").
- [ ] **Hook starts with the point**, not with "I".
- [ ] **Post is under 350 words**.
- [ ] **Read aloud test** — it sounds like you texting a friend, not a marketing team.
